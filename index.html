How to read it. Snow is the model's 24-hour total at each forecast point. First chair is what falls between 4 PM the evening before and 9 AM. Sky and gusts cover lift hours, 9 AM to 4 PM. Shaded columns are days 8 to 15, where forecast skill drops off; treat them as a trend, not a plan.

Chains. A forecast of snow on the highest pass along each route, not actual Caltrans controls. "Likely" means at least half an inch during the drive up (5–11 AM) or home (2–8 PM), or an inch overnight before the drive up, with temperatures cold enough to stick. "Possible" means lighter snow in those windows. Hover a cell to see which pass and window set it off. For what's posted right now, check Caltrans QuickMap.

What this isn't. These are raw model numbers with no local correction or forecaster judgment. Global models tend to smooth out the Sierra crest's extra snowfall in big storms, and model cloud cover counts thin high cloud, so "overcast" can still feel bright.
